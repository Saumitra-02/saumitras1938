In the evening, I also got a haircut.

## What Went Well?

- Breaking down work into smaller tasks has helped. I'll write more about this in another blog post.
- I was able to work without music.
- I managed to make continuous progress for reasonably long stretches.

## What Could I Have Done Better?

I could have worked more on both Saturday and Sunday.

**How?** I simply needed to get started and keep going, even when I felt like doing something else.

This brings me to the main question:

## What Is the Biggest Barrier?

Getting started.

I need to get started more quickly.

There's a saying that the heaviest weight at the gym is the front door. Similarly, I think starting work, regardless of how slowly I begin, is my biggest obstacle.

Once I get started, continuing is comparatively easier.

## How Can I Get Started More Quickly?

I've now figured out how to wake up early and stay awake: I can read a book that I enjoy.

However, putting the book down and starting my morning chores is still a problem.

I'll try the approach described in [Listening to the Audiobook Instead of Reading](https://saumitra-02.github.io/saumitras1938/posts/listening_to_the_audiobook_instead_of_reading/) to address this.

## How Can I Avoid Getting Distracted While Working?

Another problem is sustaining my work sessions. Sometimes, I get distracted in the middle of working and end up wasting time.

When does this happen?

- When I think the task is particularly difficult.
- When I feel I'm still far from completing the task.

In other words, I seem to get discouraged when the work ahead feels too large or difficult.

### The Solution: Keep Breaking Down the Work

I need to continue breaking tasks down into smaller steps and marking those micro-steps as complete.

Importantly, I should keep doing this even after I've entered a state of flow, even if I feel completing microtasks is slowing me down.
